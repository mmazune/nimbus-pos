The table tile

One tile for every role. It answers four things at a glance: which table, what state, whose it is, how long and how much.

Roomy · 108 px tall · used when the whole area fits the screen

Free. Tap to start an order.

Open, yours. White card, "You" in bold. Tap to add to it.

Open, someone else's. Flat and tinted, their name. A waiter can look, not change.

Not sent. Items are on the order but the kitchen has not got them.

Late. Food or drinks have passed the venue's time limit.

Bill. The bill is printed and waiting for payment.

Reserved. Shows the time and party. Tap to seat them.

Cleaning. Not ready yet. Supervisors can free it.

Compact · 84 px tall · used when the area does not fit, and on phones

A tab · for guests with no table, on busy nights

A tab works like a table with no seat. It gets the next number, lives in the Tabs area and closes when it is paid. The venue can open as many as it needs.

What is on the tile

Table name, 26 px extra-bold: the thing staff look for first.

State, always a word in a pill. Red, amber and green mean a state and nothing else.

Guests and time open, then who has it and the total in shillings.

Never on a tile: a guest's name, an order number, a code.

The rule for a 60-table venue

The floor shows one area at a time, so a venue with 64 tables in four areas never scrolls on a tablet. "All areas" fits on a big monitor. Where an area is bigger than the screen, tiles go compact first, then the area scrolls down. Nothing ever scrolls sideways.

A waiter opens on "My tables", so their own six or eight tables are always on the first screen.
